# The ladder got shorter

The median worker in Malaysia earned RM1,500 a month in 2010. In 2025, RM2,940.

Nearly double. Adjusted for prices it is still half again: RM1,300 in 2010 ringgit became RM1,978. That is 2.8 per cent a year, every year, for fifteen years.

It is a good number. It is also the number every report leads with, and it hides the thing that actually changed.

## Nineteen rooms

Think of the economy as nineteen rooms, one for each industry. In each room, line the workers up by pay and look at the one standing in the middle.

In 2010 the middle person in the best-paid room earned 3.6 times the middle person in the worst-paid room. By 2025, 3.3 times.

The rooms moved toward each other. Not far, and not in a straight line, but the direction holds up: the higher a room started, the further it fell.

Mining, 1.87 times the national median in 2010, 1.57 in 2025. Real estate, 1.67 to 1.29. Finance, 1.67 to 1.42. Electricity, 1.53 to 1.27.

And from below. Agriculture, 0.52 to 0.62. Food and accommodation, 0.60 to 0.68. Admin support, 0.67 to 0.73.

Ten rooms went up. Nine went down. The middle barely moved at all.

## The exception

One room broke the pattern, and it broke it in the direction nobody would guess.

Education started high, at 1.87 times the national median, and finished higher, at 2.04. It is the only sector in the top five that did not fall. Nine hundred and fifty-two thousand people work in it, one in eleven of everyone employed.

It is also the flattest room in the building. The average education wage is 6 per cent above the median education wage. In finance the gap is 19 per cent. In agriculture, 42.

So the education sector does not have a few people earning a great deal. It has a great many people earning more than everyone else.

## What the data cannot say

Why the rooms converged is not in this dataset.

Three explanations fit. A statutory wage floor rose over the period, lifting the bottom. A commodity and property cycle deflated the top. Public pay revisions lifted health, education and public administration, which sit on the rising side.

The data supports all three. It does not choose between them. Any page that picks one is arguing, not reporting.

## What it means

If you hire in Malaysia, the benchmark moved. The offer that beat the market in 2010 may not beat it now, and which sector you hire from matters less than it used to.

The country did not get poorer. It got flatter.
