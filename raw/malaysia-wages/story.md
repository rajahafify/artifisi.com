# Story

> **Superseded on 2026-10-26. This is version 1, and its central claim was withdrawn.**
> It was built from two snapshots, 2010 and 2025. The full 16-year series shows the public
> sector peaked in 2020 and fell back, so most of the apparent pull is one pandemic year.
> `story-v2.md` is the canonical story. This file is kept for the audit trail. See
> `wiki/contradictions.md`.

## The claim

The sectors pulling ahead of the national median are the ones the government pays for. The sectors falling behind are the private, capital-heavy ones.

## Evidence

Ratio of each sector's median wage to the national median, 2010 to 2025.

| Sector | 2010 | 2025 | Drift |
|---|---|---|---|
| Health | 1.33 | 1.69 | +0.36 |
| Public administration | 1.36 | 1.57 | +0.21 |
| Education | 1.87 | 2.04 | +0.17 |
| Real estate | 1.67 | 1.29 | -0.38 |
| Mining | 1.87 | 1.57 | -0.30 |
| Electricity | 1.53 | 1.27 | -0.27 |
| Finance | 1.67 | 1.42 | -0.24 |
| Information and communication | 1.67 | 1.61 | -0.06 |

National median: RM1,500 in 2010, RM2,940 in 2025.

## The counterpoint

Four of the biggest fallers are small employers. Mining covers 80,800 workers, electricity 98,700, real estate 83,700. A drift of -0.30 in a sector that size is a weaker signal than the same drift across finance's 419,500.

And the claim is about who employs these sectors, not proof of cause. Health, education and public administration gained for reasons the data cannot separate: civil service pay revisions, minimum wage changes, and a shift in the mix of jobs inside each sector.

## The so what

If you hire in Malaysia, the benchmark your candidate compares you against moved up in the public sectors and down in finance, real estate and utilities. A private offer that beat the market in 2010 does not automatically beat it now.

The second finding is the one most readers will not expect: information and communication, the sector everyone assumes is winning, grew slower than the national median, 89% against 96% in nominal terms, and its premium fell from 1.67x to 1.61x.
