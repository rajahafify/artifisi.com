# Story

## The claim

Malaysia's inflation eased to 1.8% in September 2026. That headline is real and it is also
close to meaningless on its own: the price level is now 37.6% above 2010, one year of that climb
is 1.8%, and inside the number the fuel is still going up while the housing is coming down.

## The evidence

Headline inflation, September 2026: **1.8%** year on year, down from 1.9% in August. Month on
month it rose 0.1%. The index is **137.6** (2010 = 100), against 135.2 a year earlier.

The thirteen divisions do not agree:

| Division | September, year on year | August | Move |
|---|---|---|---|
| Personal Care, Social Protection & Misc. | 2.8% | 3.2% | −0.4 |
| Alcoholic Beverages & Tobacco | 2.8% | 2.8% | 0.0 |
| Transport | 2.5% | 2.0% | **+0.5** |
| Restaurant & Accommodation | 2.2% | 2.1% | +0.1 |
| Education | 2.1% | 1.9% | +0.2 |
| Food & Beverages | 1.8% | 1.9% | −0.1 |
| Information & Communication | 1.8% | 2.6% | −0.8 |
| Housing, Utilities, Gas & Other Fuels | 1.4% | 2.1% | **−0.7** |
| Health | 1.4% | 1.3% | +0.1 |
| Recreation, Sport & Culture | 1.2% | 1.1% | +0.1 |
| Insurance & Financial Services | 1.2% | 1.0% | +0.2 |
| Household Furnishings, Equipment & Maintenance | 0.3% | 0.4% | −0.1 |
| Clothing & Footwear | 0.2% | 0.1% | +0.1 |

The fuel behind the transport line: the weekly market price of RON95 averaged **RM4.18** in
September 2026, against **RM2.62** in December 2025, a rise of 59.7%. Over the same period the
subsidised price did not move at all: RM2.05, with RM1.99 for BUDI 95 vehicles.

Two things the headline hides. First, geography: the state rates run from **0.5%** in Sarawak to
**2.4%** in Negeri Sembilan and Labuan, with eight states above the national 1.8% and one
exactly on it. Second, who carries the shock: urban transport prices rose **2.75%** year on year
while rural transport prices rose **1.35%**, yet the overall urban and rural rates are only 0.22
points apart (1.76% against 1.54%).

## The counterpoint

**The headline is noise at this scale.** The national rate has sat inside a band of 1.1% to 2.0%
for the whole of the last three years. A tenth of a point is well inside the series' own
month-to-month swing, so "eased to 1.8%" should not be read as a turn in anything.

**The CPI is an average of a basket whose weights are not in these datasets.** The page can say
that transport prices rose faster than housing prices. It cannot say how much of the headline
either one caused, and it does not try.

**The fuel numbers are prices, not volumes.** The transport acceleration is real at the pump, but
the dataset cannot say how much fuel is bought at the market price rather than the subsidised
one, so it cannot say how much of that 2.5% a given household carries.

**And the fuel market price is only a year old.** RON95 was fixed at RM2.05 until 30 September
2025. Every "how much it has risen" figure on this page starts from December 2025, after the
market float began, and the page says so.

## The so what

If you plan from the headline you will plan from the wrong number. The composition moved more
in one month than the headline did: housing down 0.7 points, information and communication down
0.8, transport up 0.5.

If you run a household budget or a business cost model in Malaysia, three numbers are worth more
than the 1.8%. The fuel wedge is RM2.13 a litre, and it is still open. The state spread is nearly
five to one. And the transport inflation that reached 2.75% in the cities reached only 1.35% in
the countryside, so the same headline describes two different economies.

None of this is a forecast. It is what one month's published data says when it is read
carefully instead of skimmed.
