# Brief

Stage 1 of `playbook/pipeline.md`.

**Topic**: What Malaysian prices did in September 2026. The Consumer Price Index rose 1.8% year
on year, down from 1.9% in August, so the headline says the price level is cooling. The
composition says something else: the fuel price was still climbing, and the price level itself
now sits 37.6% above 2010.

**Audience**: Malaysian households budgeting for the next three months, and anyone who has to
explain a price change to a customer, a supplier or an employee. The secondary audience is
Malaysian readers who saw "inflation eased to 1.8%" in the news and want to know whether that
means anything for their own bill.

**Question they arrive with**: prices are going up slower now, so has the squeeze let up?

**Why now**: DOSM published the September 2026 Consumer Price Index on 19 October 2026, the most
recent release. The transport line accelerated in the same month that the headline eased, and the
weekly fuel price series shows why. The full picture was not in any one number in the release.

**What people search for**: "malaysia inflation", "CPI malaysia 2026", "dosm consumer price
index", "malaysia inflation september 2026", "ron95 price malaysia". Every figure on the page is
plain text, so the page answers the search that sent someone to it.

**Pass**: the audience is named, and the question they arrive with is named.

**What this report is not**: it is not a forecast, and it does not explain why any price moved.
The CPI gives price indices and inflation rates; it does not give weights, so the page never
claims how much a given movement contributed to the headline.
