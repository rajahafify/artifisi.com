Thirteen bars, sorted by September rate. The tick on each bar is where that division stood in August. 03 The headline has not moved in three years, because two shocks have been cancelling. Since October 2023 the national rate has stayed inside a band of nine tenths of a point. It was 1.8% in October 2023 and it is 1.8% in September 2026. In between it touched 1.1% in June 2025 and 2.0% in May 2026. Underneath, core inflation has been sliding. Core is the same basket with two kinds of price removed: the ones with volatile prices, and the ones the government administers. It was 2.3% in December 2025 and 1.7% in September 2026, a fall of six tenths of a point in nine months, while the headline went from 1.6% to 1.8%. Both of the excluded kinds moved hard this year. Fuel is volatile and it rose; electricity and water tariffs are administered, and housing fell. Core strips out both, so it glides down to 1.7% while the headline stands still. The 1.8% is the net of two shocks pointing in opposite directions. The rate is a national average of a fixed basket. Dashed rails mark the band the headline never left. 04 The fuel shock is a subsidy wedge, and the wedge is still open. The market price of RON95 averaged RM4.18 a litre in September 2026, up from RM2.62 in December 2025. That is a rise of 59.7% in nine months. Over the same period the subsidised price did not move at all: RM2.05, with RM1.99 for BUDI 95 vehicles. The gap between them is RM2.13 a litre. That is the difference between what the fuel costs and what the subsidised price is, and it is the part of the fuel shock that never reached the pump price most people pay. Diesel tells the same story with a wider spread. At the market it was RM5.07 on 22 October 2026. The BUDI price was RM2.10, the SKDS price RM2.15, and in East Malaysia diesel was RM2.15. The market price of RON95 only exists from 30 September 2025; before that RON95 was fixed at RM2.05. Every "how much it has risen" figure here starts from December 2025. These are prices, not volumes, so the data cannot say how much fuel is bought at the market price. RON95 RM4.18 RM2.05 subsidised RM2.13 you do not pay 22 Oct: RM4.32 REPLAY CLICK TO PLAY Fig 4. In September, inflation ran at 0.5% in Sarawak and 2.4% in Negeri Sembilan and in Labuan. Eight states were above the national 1.8%, eight were below it, and Kuala Lumpur sat exactly on it. The full range, in order: Sarawak 0.5%, Perlis 0.6%, Kelantan 0.7%, Perak 1.1%, Terengganu 1.2%, Melaka 1.5%, Pulau Pinang 1.5%, Kuala Lumpur 1.8%, Sabah 1.9%, Selangor 1.9%, Pahang 2.1%, Johor 2.2%, Kedah 2.2%, Putrajaya 2.2%, Negeri Sembilan 2.4%, Labuan 2.4%. It is the same national basket, priced in seventeen places. What the spread measures is how differently that basket costs from one state to the next. A state rate is not a household rate. The dashed line is the national rate. 06 The same fuel shock does not land evenly. Cities carried twice the transport inflation. Urban transport prices rose 2.75% in the year to September. Rural transport prices rose 1.35%. The same fuel price, two very different transport lines. Yet the overall rates are only 0.22 points apart: urban 1.76%, rural 1.54%. A 1.40 point gap inside transport and a 0.22 point gap overall means transport carries a smaller weight in the rural basket than in the urban one. That is as far as the published data goes. Food tells a smaller version of the same story, 1.85% in the cities against 1.59% in the countryside. And the low-income basket as a whole rose 1.48%, below the national 1.78%. The urban, rural and low-income series publish an index and not a rate, so every rate in this paragraph is computed from the published index rows for September 2026 and September 2025. That includes the national 1.78% it is compared against, which is the index-derived twin of the published 1.8% headline. Data as of September 2026, retrieved 27 October 2026. Licence CC BY 4.0. DEFINITIONS Headline inflation — the year-on-year change in the consumer price index for all items. Core inflation — the same basket with items that have volatile prices or government-administered prices removed. Index — the price level with the 2010 year set to 100. A level, not a rate. Computed rates — the urban, rural and low-income figures, and the national figure they are compared against, derived from published index rows for September 2026 and September 2025. Monthly fuel price — the mean of the weekly market-price rows in that month. CAVEATS The CPI is an average. DOSM's own caveat is that the rate a specific individual experiences may be higher or lower than the published number. These datasets do not publish the basket weights, so no figure here claims how much a division contributed to the headline. The market price of RON95 exists only from 30 September 2025.
