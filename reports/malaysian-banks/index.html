Malaysia's banks, six ways

The TAB Global 1000 for 2026 ranks the world's banks by assets, and twelve of them are Malaysian. Size is one of six numbers in the table. The other five tell a different story.

  • US$950.5bassets of all twelve Malaysian banks, FY2025
  • 62%of those assets sit in Maybank, CIMB and Public Bank
  • 89%DBS's profit as a share of all twelve banks' profit
  • 3.8%return on assets at Indonesia's BCA. Malaysia's best is 1.4%

Each chart plays when you click or tap it. Every number is also in the text and in a table under the chart.

Three banks hold 62% of the assets

Maybank, CIMB and Public Bank hold US$589.7 billion between them. That is 62% of the US$950.5 billion held by all twelve Malaysian banks in the ranking.

Add RHB and Hong Leong Financial Group and the top five hold 80%. The other seven banks split the last fifth. The smallest of them, Bank Muamalat, holds 1.2%.

Maybank alone is 27.3% of the total. It ranks 116th in the world.

Size and profit are not the same order

Public Bank holds 14.6% of the twelve banks' assets and makes 18.5% of their profit. Hong Leong Financial Group holds 8.9% and makes 11.8%.

At the other end, AFFIN holds 3.2% of the assets and makes 1.3% of the profit. MBSB holds 1.5% and makes 0.4%. Maybank makes the most money of any Malaysian bank, US$2.53 billion, almost exactly its share of the assets.

A bank whose profit share is bigger than its asset share earns more on each dollar it holds. That is its return on assets, and it is drawn on its own in the last chart.

Five banks earn under 7.5% on equity

Public Bank has the best return on equity of the twelve, 12.3%. Maybank and CIMB follow at 11.2%. Seven banks are at 9.9% or more.

Five are well below that: Bank Islam at 7.1%, Bank Muamalat at 6.2%, Bank Rakyat at 4.9%, AFFIN at 4.5% and MBSB at 2.2%. Four of the five are Islamic banks. They are also among the smallest by assets.

Every Malaysian bank in the ranking made a profit. The lowest return, MBSB's 2.2%, is still above zero.

Hong Leong spends 30 sen to earn a ringgit

Cost-to-income says how much of each ringgit of income goes on running the bank. Hong Leong Financial Group's is 30.0%, the lowest of the twelve. Public Bank is next at 34.8%.

AFFIN spends 68.2 sen of every ringgit, Bank Islam 66.0 and Bank Muamalat 63.9. The big three commercial banks sit in the middle, between 34.8 and 46.8.

Lower is better here. TAB gives the ratio as a percentage. Sen per ringgit is the same number.

DBS alone earns 89% of what all twelve earn

Singapore's DBS holds US$697.8 billion. That is 73% of the assets of all twelve Malaysian banks together. It made US$8.37 billion in profit, 89% of the twelve banks' US$9.37 billion.

Singapore's three local banks, DBS, OCBC and UOB, hold US$1.67 trillion between them, 1.75 times all twelve Malaysian banks. The usual headline compares Malaysia with China's ICBC, which is eight times bigger. Singapore is the comparison next door.

DBS ranks 59th in the world. Maybank, the largest Malaysian bank, ranks 116th.

Indonesia's banks earn more on every dollar

Bank Central Asia earns 3.8% on its assets. BRI earns 2.7% and Mandiri 2.4%. The best in Malaysia is Hong Leong Financial Group at 1.4%.

Indonesia's big banks are smaller than Malaysia's. Mandiri, the largest, holds US$169.4 billion against Maybank's US$259.5 billion. They earn far more on each dollar they hold. Thailand's big four look most like Malaysia's, between 1.0% and 1.4%. Singapore's three sit between 0.9% and 1.3%.

These are the biggest local banks in each country: Malaysia's top five by assets, Singapore's three, and Thailand's and Indonesia's top four.

Method and source

Source

TAB Insights (The Asian Banker), World's 1000 Largest Banks 2026, read on 29 October 2026. It ranks banks by total assets and also gives net profit, return on equity, return on assets, cost-to-income and capital adequacy.

The data is for FY2025. Financial years ending up to March 2026 count if results were out by 31 August 2026.

What we worked out

  • Shares are each bank's assets or profit over the total for the twelve Malaysian banks: US$950,546 million in assets and US$9,372 million in profit.
  • Ratios (return on equity, return on assets, cost-to-income) are TAB's own figures. We did not recalculate them.
  • Shares are rounded to one decimal, so they may not add to exactly 100.

Caveats

  1. The free table shows ranks 1 to 914. A Malaysian bank ranked 915 to 1,000 would be missing here. The bank at 898 holds US$9.8 billion, so only a bank smaller than that could be missed.
  2. Figures are in US dollars at TAB's conversion. A move in the ringgit changes them without the banks changing.
  3. Hong Leong Financial Group and AMMB Holdings are holding groups, so their figures cover more than the bank. The others are reported at group level too.
  4. Maybank Singapore (rank 511) and Maybank Indonesia (rank 836) are counted under their own countries, as TAB does.
