# Palettes

The registry of palettes already used, so a new report does not copy one.

One report, one palette. Two reports from the same publisher share a house style, not a skin.
The family is carried by the frame, not the colour: the same mono kicker, the same caption
band, the same hairline, the same three type roles. The ground, the ink and the two accents
belong to the subject and belong to one report only.

## The registry

| Report | Subject | Picked from | Ground | Accents | Display |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `malaysia-wages` | DOSM Salaries and Wages Survey, sixteen years, nineteen industries | the subject, chosen by hand | warm cream `#f6f2ea` | green `#1e5b49` against brown ink `#332d26` | Fraunces |
| `malaysia-cpi` | DOSM Consumer Price Index, September 2026, seven series | a fuel price board at night | near-black `#0e1114` | blue `#2e86de` and red `#e8452c` | Space Grotesk |

Both are in the family and neither is the other. Put the two pages side by side on a phone and
there is no doubt which is which.

## How to pick one

The picker is a reference image. Find a photograph of the thing the report is about, the kind a
reader would have seen that morning, and take the palette off it.

1. Image search the subject. Several candidates, not one.
2. Read the dominant colours off the best ones, as hex, largest area of frame first.
3. Keep one that is unmistakably not a palette already in the registry.

Then build the working palette from it. It needs:

| Role | What it is |
|---|---|
| ground | the page background, the colour most of the frame |
| panel | a raised area, one step off the ground |
| ink | body text, the strongest value on the ground |
| muted | secondary text |
| rule | the hairline between sections |
| dimmed | axis and scale labels |
| accent | the subject |
| comparison | the other half of the story |

The two accents are not decoration. They are the two halves of the story, and they should be
chosen together rather than picked and then matched.

## What the subject has to earn

A dark ground is a choice, not a default. The CPI report earns it because its loudest beat is a
fuel price board at night, and the palette is that object. It is not the house navy and it is
not coral.

The same goes the other way. A warm cream ground suits an argument about people and a
wireframe drawing of their working lives. It does not suit a price index, and reusing it made
the CPI report look like the wages report with a different accent.

## How to check it worked

Two tests, both cheap.

- Put the new frames next to the old ones. If you cannot tell at a glance which report is
  which, the palette is not different enough.
- Read the ground colour on its own. If it is recognisable as another report's ground, stop.
